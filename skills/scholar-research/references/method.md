# Research method

## Frame the question

Record the decision the research should inform, target audience, geography, language, date window, platform scope, and explicit exclusions in `run.json`. Build a small query matrix from the core topic, synonyms, audience vocabulary, competing terms, and problem-led phrases. Avoid expanding into adjacent questions unless they change the requested decision.

## Collect in passes

The first pass maps vocabulary, communities, creators, recurring claims, and candidate trends. The second pass tests those candidates with narrower queries, counterexamples, older baselines, and direct sources. Log unsuccessful queries as coverage evidence instead of silently discarding them.

For each item capture its direct URL, platform, title, author when public and relevant, publication time, observation time, query, visible engagement snapshot, short factual summary, and optional notes. Do not copy more content than needed for analysis.

## Assess evidence

Judge a candidate trend on four separate dimensions: velocity, breadth, persistence, and relevance. The helper's score is a prioritization heuristic based on recency and log-scaled visible engagement. It cannot establish velocity without a baseline, and it must not be compared directly between platforms.

Look for coordinated reposting, duplicated articles, engagement bait, paid promotion, sampling bias, and algorithmic personalization. Count syndicated or copied material as one underlying source where possible.

## Verify and cite

Social posts reveal attention and language, but usually do not verify external factual claims. Trace product, scientific, legal, financial, and numerical claims to primary or authoritative sources. Cite direct URLs next to the statement they support and label analytical judgments as inference.

## Synthesize

Cluster semantically related items without erasing meaningful disagreement. Prefer a small number of well-supported findings over exhaustive mention counts. For every major finding explain what is happening, who is driving it, evidence strength, platform differences, why it matters, and what would falsify or weaken it.
